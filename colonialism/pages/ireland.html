Ireland (Republic)

Independent Republic

Ireland was under English/British rule for over 700 years. Following the 1916 Rising and War of Independence, 26 counties gained independence as the Irish Free State in 1922, becoming a fully independent republic in 1949.

Key events

1169
Anglo-Norman invasion of Ireland. Beginning of English involvement.
1610
Plantation of Ulster — Protestant settlers displace native Irish Catholics.
1798
United Irishmen rebellion, inspired by the French Revolution, brutally suppressed.
1845
Great Famine begins. 1 million die; 1 million emigrate in two years.
1916
Easter Rising. Proclamation of an Irish Republic. Leaders executed.
1919
Irish War of Independence begins.
1922
Irish Free State established. 26 counties independent; 6 remain British.
1949
Republic of Ireland Act. Ireland leaves the Commonwealth.

Atrocities & injustices

⚠ What the history books often omit

The Great Famine remains the defining trauma. Ireland's population fell from about 8.5 million in 1845 to roughly 6.5 million by 1851 — through both death and emigration — and has never returned to pre-Famine levels (today around 5 million in the Republic, 1.9 million in Northern Ireland). During the War of Independence, the British deployed the Black and Tans and Auxiliaries — RIC reinforcements notorious for reprisal attacks on civilians. The Kilmichael ambush, the Burning of Cork, and the Croke Park massacre of 21 November 1920 (where Crown forces — RIC and Auxiliaries — fired into a crowd at a Gaelic football match, killing 14) are among the documented atrocities. Note this is a separate event from the 1972 Bloody Sunday in Derry.

Today

The present

Ireland is a prosperous, independent EU member state. It has one of the fastest-growing economies in Europe, in part due to its low corporation tax attracting US multinationals. The Irish diaspora numbers 70–80 million worldwide — a direct legacy of famine-era emigration. There is broad public support for eventual Irish reunification, and formal planning discussions have begun on both sides of the border.
