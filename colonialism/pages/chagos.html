Chagos Islands / BIOT

British Overseas Territory (disputed)

The British Indian Ocean Territory includes the Chagos Archipelago, home to Diego Garcia — a strategically vital US military base leased from Britain. To create the territory in 1965, Britain secretly and illegally expelled the entire native Chagossian population. This is one of the most clear-cut cases of ethnic cleansing by Britain in the modern era.

Key events

1814
Chagos Islands ceded from France to Britain.
1965
Britain carves BIOT out of Mauritius before granting Mauritius independence — against the UN principle that territories should not be divided before decolonisation.
1967
Chagossians begin being forcibly removed. Their dogs are gassed. They are shipped to Mauritius and Seychelles with a suitcase each.
1971
Last Chagossians removed. US begins construction of Diego Garcia base.
2019
International Court of Justice rules the separation of Chagos from Mauritius was illegal.
2024
UK government announces a deal to cede sovereignty of Chagos to Mauritius; the treaty was signed in May 2025, with Britain retaining the Diego Garcia base under a 99-year lease.

Atrocities & injustices

⚠ What the history books often omit

The expulsion of the Chagossians is a case of deliberate ethnic cleansing carried out in secret. A 1966 Foreign Office cable referred to the islanders as 'some few Tarzans or Man Fridays.' The expelled population — several thousand people — were dumped in Mauritius with nothing, many dying in poverty. British courts have repeatedly ruled against allowing their return. The Diego Garcia base has also been implicated in CIA rendition flights.

Today

The present

The Chagossians remain in exile, mostly in Mauritius, Seychelles, and the UK. Britain agreed to hand sovereignty to Mauritius — announced in 2024 and formalised in a treaty signed in May 2025 — while the Diego Garcia base continues under a 99-year lease. Many Chagossians criticised the deal for again deciding their fate without adequately consulting them.
