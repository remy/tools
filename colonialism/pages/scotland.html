Scotland

Devolved Nation (UK)

Scotland entered a union with England in 1707 through the Acts of Union — controversial at the time, with widespread accusations of bribery of Scottish parliamentarians. The Highland Clearances of the 18th–19th centuries forcibly removed thousands of Scots to make way for sheep farming. Scotland voted 55–45 to remain in the UK in 2014.

Key events

1707
Acts of Union merge the Scottish and English Parliaments. The Scottish Parliament is dissolved.
1746
Battle of Culloden — the last pitched battle on British soil. The Jacobite rising is crushed. The Act of Proscription that follows bans the wearing of Highland dress (the Dress Act) and disarms the clans; the clan system is dismantled by the abolition of heritable jurisdictions.
1750
Beginning of the Highland Clearances. Landlords evict thousands of tenant farmers to make way for sheep.
1999
Scottish Parliament reconvened after 292 years.
2014
Scottish independence referendum. 55.3% voted to remain in the UK.
2016
Scotland votes 62% to Remain in the EU. UK votes Leave overall. SNP argues this breaks the terms of the 2014 referendum.

Atrocities & injustices

⚠ What the history books often omit

The Highland Clearances (roughly 1750–1860) involved the forced eviction of tens of thousands of Gaelic-speaking Highlanders from ancestral lands. Homes were burned, people were put on boats — many to Canada, Australia, and the US — so that landowners (often absentee English aristocracy) could run more profitable sheep farms. Entire communities were destroyed. In some areas, the population never recovered. The Gaelic language, already suppressed after Culloden, declined sharply as a result.

Today

The present

Scotland has a devolved Parliament with significant powers. The SNP has governed since 2007 and continues to push for a second independence referendum, which Westminster refuses to grant. Around 1% of Scots can speak Gaelic (2022 census), but only about 0.1% record it as their main language; there are active revival efforts. The relationship with Westminster is deeply strained, particularly post-Brexit.
