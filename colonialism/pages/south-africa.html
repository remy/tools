South Africa

Independent Republic

Britain colonised the Cape Colony in 1806 and eventually the whole of southern Africa following the Anglo-Boer Wars. The discovery of gold and diamonds drove enormous exploitation. The apartheid system (1948–1994), while created by the Afrikaner National Party, was built on British colonial foundations of racial segregation.

Key events

1806
Britain takes the Cape Colony from the Dutch (Batavian Republic).
1867
Diamonds discovered at Kimberley. Gold in the Rand in 1886. Intensification of colonial exploitation.
1899
Second Boer War begins. Britain uses concentration camps in which roughly 26,000–28,000 Boer women and children die. At least 14,000–20,000 Black Africans also died in separate, poorly recorded camps — fewer in absolute terms but at comparable or higher death rates.
1910
Union of South Africa — a self-governing dominion. Black Africans largely excluded from political rights.
1948
Apartheid formally begins under the National Party — but built on colonial-era segregation laws.
1994
First democratic elections. Nelson Mandela becomes president.

Atrocities & injustices

⚠ What the history books often omit

The concentration camps of the Boer War were among the first uses of mass civilian internment by Britain. Death rates were catastrophic. The colonial system of land dispossession — taking the most fertile land from indigenous Africans — directly created the racialised poverty that apartheid then entrenched. Britain was slow to act against apartheid internationally and maintained trade relations long after much of the world imposed sanctions.

Today

The present

South Africa is a democracy but remains one of the most unequal societies on Earth — a direct legacy of colonial and apartheid land and wealth distribution. The ANC has governed since 1994. Land redistribution remains deeply contentious.
