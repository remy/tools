Northern Ireland

Contested — Part of UK

The plantation of Ulster in the 17th century brought Protestant settlers from Britain to displace the native Catholic Irish population. When Ireland gained independence in 1922, six northern counties remained part of the UK. Decades of conflict known as 'The Troubles' killed over 3,500 people. The status of Northern Ireland remains contested.

Key events

1610
Plantation of Ulster. Native Irish Catholics are dispossessed of land; Protestant settlers from England and Scotland are installed.
1798
United Irish Rebellion crushed. Leads to the Acts of Union 1800 absorbing Ireland into the UK.
1845
The Great Famine begins. One million die; another million emigrate. British government response widely considered inadequate or actively harmful.
1916
Easter Rising in Dublin. Leaders executed by British authorities, turning public opinion towards independence.
1921
Partition of Ireland. 26 counties become the Irish Free State; 6 counties remain as Northern Ireland within the UK.
1969
The Troubles begin. Civil rights protests met with violence; British Army deployed.
1972
Bloody Sunday. British paratroopers shoot dead 13 unarmed civil rights marchers in Derry; a 14th dies months later of his wounds.
1998
Good Friday Agreement. Power-sharing arrangement; end of most armed conflict.

Atrocities & injustices

⚠ What the history books often omit

The Great Famine (1845–1852) is central to Irish memory of British rule. Approximately one million people died of starvation and disease while Ireland continued to export food to Britain. The British government's response — driven by laissez-faire ideology and, some historians argue, deliberate neglect — is considered by many to constitute a genocide. During the Troubles, both state and paramilitary actors committed atrocities. Bloody Sunday (1972), where British paratroopers shot dead 13 peaceful protesters in Derry (a 14th died later of his injuries), was the defining moment of British state violence. The Saville Inquiry (2010) found the killings were 'unjustified and unjustifiable.'

Today

The present

Northern Ireland has a power-sharing Assembly at Stormont, though it has been suspended multiple times. The population is now roughly evenly split between those who identify as unionist/British and nationalist/Irish. A majority now favour Irish unification in some polls. Brexit created a hard political border issue that remains unresolved and reignited border identity debates.
