Malta

Independent Republic

Malta was under British rule from 1800 to 1964. Its strategic location in the Mediterranean made it vital to British naval power. The island endured one of the most concentrated bombing campaigns of WWII, for which it was awarded the George Cross collectively — the only such award to an entire population, now shown on Malta's flag.

Atrocities & injustices

⚠ What the history books often omit

British colonial rule in Malta involved significant economic exploitation and cultural paternalism. Maltese petitions for greater self-governance were repeatedly denied or delayed for over a century.

Today

The present

Malta is an independent EU member state and republic, with a strong tourism economy and growing financial sector. The Maltese language — descended from Siculo-Arabic with heavy Sicilian/Italian and English influence — has survived, and is the only Semitic language that is an official EU language and the only one written in the Latin script.
