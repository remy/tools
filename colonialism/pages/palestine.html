Palestine / Israel

Ongoing Conflict — British Mandate Legacy

Britain controlled Palestine from 1920 under a League of Nations Mandate. The Balfour Declaration (1917) promised a homeland for Jewish people in Palestine while also promising to protect the rights of existing Arab communities — commitments that proved impossible to reconcile. Britain's withdrawal in 1948 led directly to the first Arab-Israeli War.

Key events

1917
Balfour Declaration — Britain pledges support for 'a national home for the Jewish people' in Palestine.
1920
British Mandate for Palestine begins.
1936
Arab Revolt against British rule and Jewish immigration. Brutally suppressed — 5,000 Arabs killed.
1946
Irgun bombing of the King David Hotel in Jerusalem — 91 killed.
1947
Britain refers the problem to the UN, which proposes partition.
1948
Britain withdraws. Israel declares independence. First Arab-Israeli War begins. 700,000 Palestinians flee or are expelled (the Nakba).

Atrocities & injustices

⚠ What the history books often omit

The Balfour Declaration made contradictory promises that Britain never resolved. During the Mandate, Britain suppressed both Arab and Jewish uprisings with significant violence. The 1936–39 Arab Revolt saw RAF bombing of Arab villages, mass detention, and house demolitions — tactics later used by Israel. Britain's abrupt withdrawal in 1948, without a resolution to the competing national claims it had encouraged, is considered one of the most consequential failures of British colonial policy.

Today

The present

The Israel-Palestine conflict is the most internationally prominent unresolved colonial legacy in the world. The UK's role in creating the conditions for it is rarely given adequate weight in British public discourse.
