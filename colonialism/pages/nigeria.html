Nigeria

Independent Republic

Nigeria — Africa's most populous country — was assembled from distinct peoples and kingdoms by Britain for administrative convenience. Independence came in 1960, but the borders Britain drew have caused immense instability, including a civil war in which up to 3 million people died.

Atrocities & injustices

⚠ What the history books often omit

The amalgamation of Nigeria was done for British administrative convenience, merging peoples with vastly different cultures, religions, and political systems. The consequences are still felt today. During the Biafran War, the British government supported the Nigerian federal government, including the use of starvation as a weapon against the Igbo population. Images of starving Biafran children sparked one of the first modern humanitarian media campaigns.

Today

The present

Nigeria is one of Africa's largest economies and its most populous nation (estimated at over 230 million people in 2024–25). It faces severe challenges: corruption, the Boko Haram insurgency in the north, Niger Delta oil conflict, and the ongoing tensions between its many ethnic and religious groups — most of which trace back to the arbitrary borders drawn by Britain.
