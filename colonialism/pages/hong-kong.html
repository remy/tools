Hong Kong

Returned to China (1997)

Britain seized Hong Kong from China after the First Opium War — a war Britain started to force China to accept opium imports. It was returned to China in 1997 under a 'one country, two systems' arrangement meant to last until 2047. That arrangement is now under severe pressure.

Atrocities & injustices

⚠ What the history books often omit

The Opium Wars are perhaps the most nakedly commercial of all British colonial enterprises — Britain fought to maintain its right to addict the Chinese population to opium grown in India and sold for profit. The Treaty of Nanking was forced at gunpoint. Britain's refusal to democratise Hong Kong during its entire 150 years of rule — finally granting more democratic rights only in the final years — left the population with few tools to protect itself after the handover.

Today

The present

Hong Kong's special status is effectively over following the 2020 National Security Law. Many pro-democracy activists have been imprisoned; hundreds of thousands have emigrated, many to the UK under a special BN(O) visa scheme Britain introduced. This is seen by some as belated responsibility for a population Britain governed for 150 years without giving them democratic rights.
